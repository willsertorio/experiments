Core loop
Core loop 1 — The player at least sees the intro scene and uses the main menu to choose at least Start, How to play, or Settings, and the choice leads to the chosen screen rather than a scripted sequence.✓The game opens on Click to begin over the dark hall. That click starts the sound and a staged intro of about fourteen seconds that ends on the BEAMLINK logo. Space, Enter, Esc or the Skip label skips it and lands on the main menu. The menu offers Start, How to Play and Settings over the hall, with no controls text. Each one opens its own screen, and Back or Esc returns to the menu.
Core loop 2 — The player at least enters a level and looks around to find at least the light source, the receiver, the exit, and the flat surfaces that can hold a portal.✓Each level opens with an eight second camera flight that passes the sun disc, the beam, the mirrors, the sun lens and the sealed doorway, then settles into the player's eyes. The portal surfaces are square bronze-framed mirrors with a round pale stone face, the palest surfaces in the cool hall. The aim ring lights over a pale mirror and shows a cross over bare stone.

Core loop 4 — The player at least fires the cyan portal and the magenta portal onto eligible flat surfaces.✓Left click places the cyan portal and right click the magenta one. Core loop 5 — The player at least watches the beam travel from the source, enter one portal, exit the other portal, and continue across the level.✓The beam is traced live: it enters one portal and leaves straight out of the face of the other. The bronze inlay under the beam glows gold as it passes, and the portal rings spin faster while the light goes through.
Core loop 6 — The player at least moves either portal and tries new positions until the beam reaches the receiver and the receiver opens the exit.✓Firing a portal again closes its petals and reopens it on the new mirror, and R clears both. When the beam reaches the sun lens, its rays unfold, a gold wave runs along the inlays to the doorway, the lamps wake and the slab sinks with sand pouring out. Moving the beam away seals the door again, and the top right lines switch from Sun lens dark and Exit sealed to Sun lens lit and Exit open.
Core loop 7 — The player at least walks through the opened exit to finish the level and move to the next level, and finishing the final level ends the game, plays the ending scene, and returns the player to the main menu.✓Walking through the open doorway raises Level Clear in bronze letters on a soft dark glow, and the next level opens with its own camera flight. The game has three levels: First Light, Turn the Beam and The Gap. Clearing Level 3 plays the ending film, which fades to black and returns to the main menu.
Presentation
Art style — The game has a deliberate 3D visual style, distinct enough to describe in one sentence, held from the first screen to the last.✓The style is one low-poly sandstone gallery where light wakes a sleeping hall. Walls are hand-laid blocks in courses, each a slightly different shade, with bevelled edges, chipped corners and cracked floor tiles. A band of darker sandstone runs along the foot of each wall. Bronze frames carry corner bolts and a soft sheen, and the exit is a carved basalt doorway with a ring band. Cool moonlight turns warm gold when the sun lens lights. The same hall, with a magenta portal and its beams, sits behind the menu, and the ending goes out to the dunes at dawn.
Animation — At least every important action produces a visible reaction — particles, a shake, a squash, a flash — timed well and not overdone.✓Each level opens with a camera flight that lands softly in the player's eyes. Portals open as stone irises with a small overshoot and a spark burst, and each disc glows from a pale center to a deep rim with a slow swirl. When the sun lens lights, its rays unfold, a gold wave runs along the inlays and the door slab sinks with sand. Falling into the Level 3 gap tilts the view into the shaft and fades it to dark teal. The ending is one long camera move along the beam that flashes cyan as it passes through the portals, then goes out over the dunes at dawn.
Audio — The game has at least music or ambience, plus sound effects at the key moments.✓The intro has its own sound: wind and a drone in the dark, a deep bell when the sun disc ignites, chimes on the portals and a warm chord under the title. In play, ambient pads and distant bells sit under a soft beam hum. Each portal color has its own chime, a refused shot fizzles, the lens lands a warm chord and the door rumbles. The ending stops the hall's chord changes and bells and plays its own slow theme, which resolves on a long chord under the title. Esc pauses every sound, and Sound off silences every screen.
UI — The interface is understood at a glance, with the most important information standing out and matching the rest of the game visually.✓The title is a drawn logo. BEAM and LINK sit in bold two-tone bronze letters with a dark outline, joined by linked cyan and magenta rings and a golden underline that jumps through them. Settings, How to Play, Paused and The light was lost share one serif and the same bronze heading with a golden beam underline. They sit on a soft dark gradient over the hall, with no cards or icon buttons. Level Clear uses the same bronze heading. The focused item turns gold as a thin golden beam slides in from the left. How to Play shows each key in a small key cap and teaches the rules with three framed pictures. In play, the level name sits top left, four status lines sit top right and the aim ring is centered.
Intro — The intro presents the premise with at least one staged scene, rather than only a title card.✓The intro is a staged scene in four beats, inside letterbox bars. In the dark hall the sun disc sparks, ignites and fires the beam into stone. The camera finds the sealed doorway, whose rings glow and die. Cyan and magenta portals bend the beam into the sun lens for a heartbeat, then snap shut, and the BEAMLINK logo lands. Three captions in the bottom bar set the premise: the beam has forgotten its way, and two linked portals can bend it.
Victory — level — Completing a level produces a moment that marks it as won, distinct from failing it.✓Winning is warm: the sun lens flares, a gold wave wakes the hall, the doorway sinks open and Level Clear rises in bronze letters with a chime. Losing is cold: a fall into the Level 3 gap tilts the view into the dark shaft and fades it to dark teal with a low falling tone. After that, The light was lost appears with Retry and Main menu.
Victory — game — The ending scene is distinct from any single level win and closes the premise the intro set up.✓The ending is a film in letterbox with its own theme. It opens close on the flaring sun disc and rides the beam into the cyan portal. A cyan flash, then a magenta one, carries it out of the second portal to the sun lens as it bursts gold. The camera follows the gold wake to the doorway and out through a stone arch over the dunes. As the sun rises, two huge cyan and magenta rings link around it, and the golden beam leaves the arch, passes through both rings and strikes the sun, which flares. The BEAMLINK logo lands below the rings with Thanks for playing, then the main menu returns.

GDD · Beamlink

Concept

A 3D puzzle game in which the player explores each level on foot, places a pair of linked portals on flat surfaces, and redirects a continuous beam of light from its source to a receiver so the powered receiver opens the level exit. The player reads the room, tests portal placements against the beam's path, and revises them until the exit opens.

The tone is calm, focused curiosity with quiet moments of revelation.

A level ends when the player walks through the opened exit, or when the player falls and the level fail screen appears. The game ends when the player completes the final level and the ending scene plays.

The core mechanic

Dual Portal System with Light Redirection. The player fires a cyan portal and a magenta portal onto eligible flat surfaces, and the two portals form a linked pair. A beam of light that enters one portal exits the other, preserving its direction relative to the exit portal's orientation.

1. Core gameplay loop

  1. The player sees the intro scene, then uses the main menu to pick Start, How to play, or Settings.
  2. The player enters a level and looks around to find the light source, the receiver, the exit, and the flat surfaces that can hold a portal.
  3. The player walks around the level to reach a position with a clear view of a surface where a portal would help.
  4. The player fires the cyan portal and the magenta portal onto eligible flat surfaces.
  5. The player watches the beam travel from the source, enter one portal, exit the other, and continue across the level.
  6. The player moves either portal and tries new positions until the beam reaches the receiver and the receiver opens the exit.
  7. The player walks through the opened exit to finish the level and move to the next level; finishing the final level ends the game, plays the ending scene, and returns the player to the main menu.

2. Controls

  • W, A, S, D — move the player around the level.
  • Mouse — look around the level.
  • Space — jump to a reachable ledge or across a gap at the same height.
  • Left mouse button — fire the cyan portal at the flat surface the player is looking at.
  • Right mouse button — fire the magenta portal at the flat surface the player is looking at.
  • R — clear both portals from the level so the player can start the placement again.
  • Esc — pause and resume during play, and go back one screen in menus.
  • The player discovers how the exit portal's facing changes the beam's new direction by placing portals and watching the beam in play.

3. Mechanics and rules

Movement. The player walks and jumps on foot through each level and cannot pass through solid walls, floors, or barriers. The player reaches portal surfaces by looking at them, and reaches the exit by walking to it.

Portal placement. Firing a portal places it on the eligible flat surface the player is looking at, and firing the same portal again moves it to the new surface. The cyan portal and the magenta portal stay linked wherever the player places them.

Eligible surfaces. A portal attaches to a flat surface that is large enough and unobstructed, and a shot at any other surface leaves both portals where they are. The player can tell before firing whether the surface being looked at will accept a portal.

Beam travel. The beam leaves the source in a straight line and continues until it reaches a portal, a solid object, the receiver, or the edge of the level. Entering one portal makes the beam exit the linked portal at once and continue in its new direction.

Exit orientation. The beam preserves its direction relative to the exit portal's orientation, so turning the exit portal turns the beam that leaves it. A beam that enters the front of one portal leaves from the front of the other portal.

Beam-only portals. The portals carry the beam of light, and the player walks past them without travelling through them. Solid objects block the beam, and the beam does not harm the player.

Receiver and exit. The receiver opens the level exit while the beam is reaching it, and the exit closes again if the beam moves away. The player completes the level by walking through the open exit.

Falling. A fall below the level fails the level and shows the level fail screen. The player can also restart the current level from the pause menu at any time.

4. Experience pillars

Onboarding

The first levels teach one idea at a time through small objectives the player completes in play: firing one portal, linking both portals, and turning the beam by choosing where the exit portal faces. Each new obstacle appears in a level that lets the player try it safely before later levels combine it with earlier ideas.

Enemies

There are no enemies; the opposition is the level itself, including at least solid objects that block the beam, surfaces that will not accept a portal, and falls below the level. Later levels combine these obstacles so the beam's path and the player's walking route pull against each other.

Rewards and power-ups

Powering the receiver and opening the exit reward the player inside a level, and the player carries the satisfaction of a solved route into the next level. The player has the linked portal pair from the start and uses it by choice, so progress comes from new ways of using the same portals rather than from collected power-ups.

Progression

The game has at least ten levels, and completing a level unlocks the next one while nothing the player places carries over between levels. Later levels make the beam travel through more of the room, leave the player fewer useful surfaces to work with, and add new obstacles to deal with, including blocking objects, unusable surfaces, and falls.

5. Interface and HUD

In play

  • Aiming marker — whether the surface the player is looking at will accept a portal, and which portal a shot would place.
  • Cyan portal status — whether the cyan portal is placed, so the player knows which half of the pair still needs a surface.
  • Magenta portal status — whether the magenta portal is placed, so the player knows which half of the pair still needs a surface.
  • Receiver status — whether the beam is reaching the receiver and the exit is open or closed.
  • Exit status — whether walking to the exit will finish the level now or the beam still needs redirecting.
  • Current level — which level the player is working on and whether to keep experimenting or restart it.

Menus

  • Intro scene — shows the title and sets up the premise of redirecting the beam to power the exit, and the player can skip it.
  • Main menu — offers at least Start, How to play, and Settings.
  • How to play screen — explains the controls and the portal and beam rules as a reference the player can open and return from.
  • Settings — offers at least sound on/off and an option that plays the ending scene directly.
  • Pause menu — offers at least Resume, sound on/off, Restart level, and Back to the main menu.
  • Level fail screen — offers at least Retry and Back to the main menu after a fall.
  • Ending scene — plays when the player beats the game, then returns the player to the main menu.
