GDD · Lean Line

Concept

Lean Line is a 2D physics motorcycle balance game where the player rides an infinitely-generated obstacle course and keeps the rider upright by shifting the rider's weight by hand. The rider stays where the player leaves the rider, so every climb, landing and drop is a balance the player holds or loses.

The tone is tense and playful, with relief at every crest cleared.

A run ends when the rider crashes and the player stops retrying from the last checkpoint. The game ends when the player clears the final level, plays the ending scene and returns to the main menu, with the option to keep riding the course as it keeps generating ahead.

The core mechanic

Manual Balance. Leaning shifts the rider's weight and the bike's centre of mass, and the rider does not return to upright on the rider's own. The player leans the other way to bring the bike back, and uses throttle, braking and leaning together on the ground and in the air to stay balanced.

1. Core gameplay loop

  1. The player sees the intro scene, then picks Start, How to play or Settings on the main menu.
  2. The player rides into the first level of the course and learns to move by riding it.
  3. The player leans forward and backward to climb, crest and descend each obstacle without tipping over.
  4. The player uses throttle and braking together with leaning to land jumps and recover from bad angles.
  5. The player reaches a checkpoint, which completes the level and starts the next level as the course generates ahead.
  6. The player crashes, sees the level fail screen, and retries the level from the last checkpoint or returns to the main menu.
  7. Finishing a level moves the player into the next level, and finishing the final level plays the ending scene and returns the player to the main menu.

2. Controls

  • Right arrow or D — lean the rider forward toward the front wheel.
  • Left arrow or A — lean the rider backward toward the rear wheel.
  • Up arrow or W — apply the throttle and drive the bike forward.
  • Down arrow or S — brake, and slow or stop the bike.
  • Space — use a held power-up.
  • Mouse — select buttons and options in menus.
  • Esc — pause and resume during play, and go back one screen in menus.

Nothing on screen teaches the player that a leaned rider stays leaned until leaned back; the player discovers this by riding the opening level.

3. Mechanics and rules

Manual Balance. Leaning moves the rider's weight over the bike and changes how the bike tips, climbs and lands. Releasing a lean control leaves the rider in the leaned position until the player leans the other way.

Throttle and Brake. The throttle drives the rear wheel and shifts weight toward the rear, and braking shifts weight toward the front. Strong throttle can lift the front wheel, and hard braking can lift the rear wheel.

Air Control. In the air, leaning rotates the bike toward the lean, and throttle and braking change the rotation of the rear wheel and the angle of the bike. The player lines the bike up with the landing slope before touchdown.

Course and Obstacles. The course generates ahead of the bike as slopes, jumps, gaps, seesaws, loops of track, narrow bridges and movable objects. Each level ends at a checkpoint gate the player rides through.

Crashes. The rider crashes when the rider's head or body strikes the ground or an obstacle, or when the bike lands in a fall the rider cannot ride out of. A crash ends the attempt at the level and shows the level fail screen.

Checkpoints. Riding through a checkpoint gate completes the level and sets the place the player retries from after a crash. A crash returns the bike and rider to the last checkpoint in the upright starting position.

4. Experience pillars

Onboarding

The opening levels teach by riding, a few ideas at a time, starting with throttle and braking on gentle ground, then leaning on climbs, then air control at a first jump. Each new obstacle appears first in a forgiving form, and the How to play screen stays available as a reference.

Enemies

There are no enemy riders; the opposition is the course itself: at least steep climbs, gaps, seesaws, narrow bridges and movable objects that tip, roll or fall under the bike's weight. Gravity and the bike's own momentum turn each obstacle into a balance the player can lose.

Rewards and power-ups

Collecting trail stars placed on risky lines pays for taking the harder balance, and reaching a checkpoint is the reward that banks the level. The player can hold at least a Stabiliser, which steadies the bike while it is active, and a Retry Token, which returns the rider to the last checkpoint upright, and the player uses each one by choice during play.

Progression

The player advances level by level, and the game has at least twelve levels. Later levels ask more of the player's balance with steeper and longer climbs, wider gaps and moving obstacles in combination, and they add new obstacles and power-ups to deal with while earlier lessons keep returning in harder forms.

5. Interface and HUD

In play

  • Balance indicator — how far the rider leans toward tipping, and whether to lean back now.
  • Level progress — how much of the current level stands between the bike and the next checkpoint.
  • Trail stars collected — whether the risky line was worth taking in this level.
  • Held power-up — which power-up the player holds and can use with Space.
  • Checkpoint notice — that the level is complete and retries now start from here.

Menus

  • Intro scene — the title and a short scene that sets up the ride, with an option to skip it.
  • Main menu — Start, How to play and Settings.
  • How to play — a reference for the controls and the manual balance rule.
  • Settings — sound on/off and an option that plays the ending scene directly.
  • Pause menu — Resume, sound on/off, Restart level and Back to the main menu.
  • Level fail screen — Retry and Back to the main menu after a crash.
  • Ending scene — the scene that plays when the player clears the final level, then returns to the main menu.
