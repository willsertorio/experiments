Presentation
Art style — The game has a deliberate 3D visual style, distinct enough to describe in one sentence, held from the first screen to the last.✓Cel shaded toy robots made of chunky armor plates with worn painted metal everywhere, chipped edges, seams and rivets, cream, orange and teal for my side and crimson for the rivals, in a stadium arena lit by warm spotlights and teal neon, and the same look on the intro, the menu, the HUD, the result panels and the ending.
Animation — At least every important action produces a visible reaction — particles, a shake, a squash, a flash — timed well and not overdone.✓Rook breathes, blinks and follows the mouse with his eye, every robot walks with real steps, each rival moves in its own way, every shot has a small pull back before the kick, hits flash white with sparks, kills blow the robot apart with a ring on the floor, taking damage shakes the screen with a red edge, and winning fires confetti while the crowd jumps.
Audio — The game has at least music or ambience, plus sound effects at the key moments.✓A calm menu theme without drums with a bell melody, and a match track that moves through intro, verse, chorus, bridge and a quiet break, with a layer that rises when anyone transforms, metal footsteps for each robot, layered sounds for shots, hits, kills, dash and the heroes, Rook chirps when he is happy or hurt, a jingle on win, a falling sting on loss, and Sound OFF mutes everything.
UI — The interface is understood at a glance, with the most important information standing out and matching the rest of the game visually.✓Every text uses the game’s own block lettering with cut corners and a slant, the new logo has a metal badge with the robot head, the scoreboard sits at the top as cream and red plates around a VS, the BP meter is the biggest thing at the bottom center with TRANSFORM [E], and every panel is the same LED plate with a teal frame.

GDD · Battlepoint Heroes

Concept

A 3D top-down arena shooter where the player fights rival combatants in enclosed arenas, earns Battle Points (BP) through combat and holding objectives, and spends BP to transform into a powerful hero unit that can overturn a losing match. Every combatant plays by the same BP rules, so an enemy hero can seize control just as the player's hero can, and the match stays in doubt until the winning score lands.

Frantic, swinging and triumphant, with defeat always one enemy transformation away.

A match ends when the player or a rival reaches the match score target shown in play. Winning a match unlocks the next match, and winning the final match beats the game and plays the ending scene.

The core mechanic

The Battle Point Hero Transformation System. Fighting, scoring eliminations and holding objectives earn the player Battle Points, shown on the BP meter. When the meter holds enough BP, the player chooses when to spend it and transform into the hero unit picked for that match. The hero unit is stronger than a standard combatant and carries a hero ability, and the transformation lasts for a limited time before the player returns to standard form with an empty BP meter. Rivals earn and spend BP the same way.

1. Core gameplay loop

  1. The player sees the intro scene, can skip it, and reaches the main menu to pick Start, How to play or Settings.
  2. The player picks a hero unit for the match and enters the arena in standard form.
  3. The player moves around the arena, aims and shoots rivals, and fights over the active objective.
  4. The player earns Battle Points from combat and objective play and watches the BP meter for the moment transformation becomes available.
  5. The player chooses when to transform, plays as the hero unit to score eliminations and take objectives, then returns to standard form when the transformation ends.
  6. The player keeps contesting the match after each return to standard form, respawns after each defeat in play, and transforms again as BP allows until the player or a rival reaches the match score target.
  7. Winning a match takes the player to the next match, losing a match takes the player to the match fail screen to retry or return to the main menu, and winning the final match finishes the whole game with the ending scene before returning to the main menu.

2. Controls

  • W, A, S, D — move the combatant around the arena.
  • Mouse — aim the combatant's weapon toward the pointer.
  • Left mouse button — fire the current form's weapon.
  • Shift — dash in the current movement direction.
  • E — transform into the picked hero unit when the BP meter holds enough BP.
  • Q — use the hero ability while transformed.
  • Esc — pause and resume during play, and go back one screen in menus.

Nothing essential is left for the player to discover without an on-screen prompt during play.

3. Mechanics and rules

Movement and dash. The player moves freely around the arena and aims independently of movement. Dashing gives a short burst of movement and needs a short recovery before the player can dash again.

Shooting and health. Shots damage the combatant they hit, and a combatant with no health left is eliminated and drops out of play until respawn. The player's health returns in full on respawn, and rivals follow the same health rules.

Battle Points. Eliminations, damaging rivals and holding the active objective earn Battle Points for the combatant who does them. Taking damage and being eliminated never remove earned BP, and the BP meter shows how close the player is to transformation.

Hero transformation. The player transforms only by choosing to spend BP in play, and the transformation lasts for a limited time shown on screen. The hero unit has more health and a stronger weapon than a standard combatant, returns to standard form when its time or health runs out, and leaves the BP meter empty either way.

Hero units. The player picks at least one hero unit before each match from the units the game offers, and each hero unit pairs a distinct weapon with a distinct hero ability the player triggers with the hero ability control. Rivals transform into the same hero units and use the same weapons and abilities.

Objectives. Arenas have objectives that combatants hold by staying on an active objective without a rival present. Holding an objective earns BP over time and adds to the holder's match score.

Scoring and respawns. Eliminations and objective holds add to a combatant's match score, and an eliminated combatant respawns at their side of the arena after a short wait. The first combatant to reach the match score target wins the match.

Match win and loss. The player wins a match by reaching the match score target before any rival. A rival reaching the target first loses the match for the player and shows the match fail screen.

4. Experience pillars

Onboarding

The first match teaches movement, aiming and shooting through small objectives the player completes in play, starting with reaching a marked spot and shooting marked targets. Holding an objective, earning BP and transforming appear next as prompted objectives in the same match, and each new hero unit, rival kind and hazard is shown in play when the player first needs to deal with it.

Enemies

The player faces rival combatants who fight for objectives, earn BP and transform under the same rules, in at least a rushing kind that closes distance and fights up close, a marksman kind that keeps distance and fires carefully, and a bulwark kind that holds objectives and soaks damage. A transformed rival is the most dangerous opponent in the arena, and rivals contest objectives instead of only chasing the player.

Rewards and power-ups

Combat and objective play pay the player in Battle Points, and BP buys the transformation that lets the player take over a match. Arenas also offer pickups the player collects in play, including health restored on collection and a BP pickup that fills part of the meter, and defeating a transformed rival pays a large BP reward that can fund the player's own transformation.

Progression

The game has at least five matches. Later matches pull the player forward with new arenas, new rival kinds and new hero units to pick, and unlocked hero units carry over between matches. Later matches get harder in more than one way: rivals fight more accurately and transform more readily, arenas add hazards and more contested objectives, and matches ask more of the player's aim, positioning and timing of transformations.

5. Interface and HUD

In play

  • Health — whether to keep fighting, dash away or fall back toward a health pickup.
  • Battle Points (BP) meter — whether to keep earning, or spend BP and transform now.
  • Transformation time remaining, while transformed — whether to push for eliminations and objectives now or use the last of the transformation safely.
  • Hero ability readiness, while transformed — whether the hero ability is ready to use on the player's next move.
  • Player score and leading rival score — whether to chase eliminations, hold the objective or stop the leading rival.
  • Active objective status — whether the objective is held, contested or free to take.
  • Current objective prompt — what the game is teaching or asking the player to do next during onboarding.
  • Rival transformation warning — whether a rival has just become a hero unit the player must avoid or focus down.

Menus

  • Intro scene — shows the title and a short scene that sets up the arena contest and the power of transformation, and offers skipping ahead.
  • Main menu — offers Start, How to play and Settings.
  • Hero pick screen — lets the player pick which available hero unit to transform into in the next match, with each unit's weapon and hero ability named in plain words.
  • How to play — lists the controls and explains Battle Points, transformation, objectives and winning a match as a reference the player can open.
  • Settings — offers sound on/off and an option that plays the ending scene directly.
  • Pause menu — offers Resume, sound on/off, Restart match and Back to the main menu.
  • Match results screen — shows whether the player won the match and offers continuing to the next match or returning to the main menu.
  • Match fail screen — offers Retry and Back to the main menu.
  • Ending scene — plays after the final match is won, then returns to the main menu.
